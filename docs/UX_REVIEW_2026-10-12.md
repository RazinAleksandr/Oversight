# Oversight: iPhone UX review and redesign proposal

12 October 2026 · Review of the signed candidate on `codex/ios-port`.

**Status: analysis and proposals only. Awaiting discussion and approval.** No game,
engine, art, localization, or iOS configuration changes were made during this review.
This is a new design decision gate, following the user's experience on their iPhone.

## Verdict

The current interface is poorly suited to sustained phone play. It asks the player
to read, remember, navigate and acknowledge too much before the strategic choice
becomes clear. Landscape has a severe space-allocation defect. Portrait works
mechanically, but decisions and their supporting information remain separated by
long scrolling journeys. The visual treatment adds competition for attention.

The port preserved the dashboard's information structure. Earlier work improved
individual controls, persistence and explanations, but did not establish a comfortable
phone interaction model. Passing a no-overflow check did not measure how much usable
space remained, whether choices could be compared, or whether a newcomer understood
the consequence of a decision. Those were gaps in the previous acceptance process.

**Recommended direction:** a portrait-first political thriller built around one
full-screen decision at a time, with contextual evidence and a quieter government
dossier identity. Keep investigations, scarce actions, competing interests and
consequences that return later. Reduce administrative reading and navigation first.

## UX fitness estimate

These are qualitative expert judgments, not measured player scores or a numerical
rating of taste. Confidence describes the evidence behind each judgment.

| Dimension | Current fitness | Evidence confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Portrait comfort | Poor for sustained play | High: rendered decisions, panels and measured scroll lengths |
| Landscape comfort | Severe usability failure | High: only 85 px of main content remains once the news strip appears |
| First-run learning | Weak | Medium-high: introduction, setup, manual and first decisions inspected; no new-player study yet |
| Comparing decisions | Weak | High: four deployment options exceed the available decision viewport |
| Understanding consequences | Mixed | High for specific defects; medium for general comprehension |
| Pacing | Too much acknowledgment and full-detail reading | Medium: source plus three deterministic workload traces and prior playtest |
| Visual hierarchy and identity | Incoherent, heavily ornamented | High for observed patterns; preference remains a design discussion |

| Accessibility on an actual iPhone | Unverified | Browser checks do not establish VoiceOver, larger-text or thumb comfort |

## The eight main findings

### 1. Persistent interface consumes the playing space

**P1 · Quality defect · High confidence**

The same tall HUD, full-width progression button and bottom tabs remain in
landscape. At 844×390, the initial main viewport is **129 px** high. After news
arrives, it becomes **85 px**: roughly **22% of the screen**. A welcome tip covers
nearly the entire remaining central area.

Opening a briefing does not reclaim the HUD's 130 px. The width-only breakpoint at
760 px then adds centered-modal spacing, leaving a **212 px** sheet. The sticky
63 px “Review choices” dock and dossier header occupy most of that space. This
reproduces the user's horizontal-screen complaint directly.

Portrait also pays a substantial cost: 390×844 leaves 539 px for the main panel
after the news strip appears, before any tip. At 320×568, a briefing has only
438 px of height while the opening dossier alone is about 1,541 px tall.

**Proposal:** decisions use the full available screen with a compact local header.
Show relevant resources and affected meters there; keep the complete national
overview on the desk and one tap away. Remove persistent news and End Quarter
controls from unrelated panels. Treat compact height as its own layout condition.

If both orientations remain supported, landscape needs a deliberate two-column
reading/decision composition. Width alone must not select the desktop modal.

Evidence: [landscape briefing](../artifacts/ux-audit-2026-10-12/en-844x390-briefing.png),
[later landscape desk](../artifacts/ux-audit-2026-10-12/en-844x390-turn10-desk.png).

`src/ui/game/GameScreen.tsx`.

### 2. A decision is presented as a long document followed by a long menu

**P1 · Quality defect · High confidence**

At 390×844, the first deployment sheet is about **2,322 px** of scrollable content.
Its choices section alone is **891 px** tall. The visible choice area shows two
complete options and the beginning of the third. The fourth requires more scrolling.
The same Russian sheet is about **2,555 px** long.

“Review choices” now provides a useful jump, but it moves the title, evidence and
context out of view. The player compares options from memory, then jumps back to
Evidence or Advisors. A title, decorative file metadata, stamp and completed
typewriter label consume space before the substantive information.

**Proposal:** the first decision view contains the actual question, a short stakes
summary and all option labels. Selecting an option expands its full known cost,
benefit, timing and uncertainty; a named confirmation commits it. Keep a concise
evidence summary alongside the decision and open detailed evidence in place.
Reversible selection adds one tap but enables comparison and reduces accidental
commitment. Test that tradeoff in the prototype.

Full narrative and advisor commentary remain available. Editing summaries must
preserve warnings, attribution, uncertainty and conflicting advice. Summaries
must use the fogged view and authored content, never hidden engine truth.

Evidence: [deployment reading](../artifacts/ux-audit-2026-10-12/en-390x844-deployment.png),
[deployment choices](../artifacts/ux-audit-2026-10-12/en-390x844-deployment-choices.png).
Source: `src/ui/game/Briefing.tsx`, `src/styles/ui-card.css`.

### 3. Onboarding requires understanding before it supplies experience

**P1 · Quality defect · Medium-high confidence**

Nation selection asks a newcomer to evaluate capability growth, confidence swings,
adoption ramps and policy costs. Difficulty defaults to Director, described as
the intended experience. The initial welcome then introduces institutional names,
survival meters, the clock and a strategic opening choice. A separate manual has
seven conceptual sections. Progressive tab unlocks exist, but an unlocked Nation
panel still exposes five funding programs, ten laws and multiple economic concepts.

These are several explanations of the game before a complete, demonstrated causal
loop. A player can acknowledge tips without gaining a usable mental model.

**Proposal:** offer a short guided first assignment, separate from Daily Run. Introduce
one deployment, one uncertain finding, the cost of investigating and a visible
consequence. Teach the quarter transition by doing it. Offer a recommended nation
with a plain-language reason; make full country modifiers optional during setup.
Experienced players retain direct access to the full setup and existing campaign.

A scripted teaching scenario changes onboarding/content and needs its own authored
EN/RU material. It should not silently change a normal run's odds or spend actions
for the player. Do not describe Analyst as merely “for bad players”; its clearer
signals make it a plausible first-run recommendation to test.

Evidence: [setup](../artifacts/ux-audit-2026-10-12/en-390x844-setup.png),
[welcome in landscape](../artifacts/ux-audit-2026-10-12/en-844x390-welcome.png).
Source: `NewGameScreen.tsx`, `HowToPlay.tsx`, `Tips.tsx`.

### 4. Related mechanics are scattered, and some labels obscure what changes

**P1 · Quality defect · High confidence**

To understand a deployment, the player may need company capability in Labs,
safeguards below five programs in Nation, adoption/targets in Society, and the
evaluation in the briefing. Navigating these locations does not itself explain
the relationship between them.

One concrete semantic problem: the choice chip says **“Adoption · Business +13”**.
`projectedGains()` and `approveModel()` actually change the **adoption target**.
Actual uptake ramps over future quarters. The result text explains the ramp after
commitment. A demand forecast elsewhere does not resolve this ambiguity at the
deployment decision.

Other terms require distinctions: government Confidence versus confidence in
evidence; safety culture versus safeguards versus oversight; evidence lines versus
spendable Evidence; risk estimate versus final control probability. The clock's
23:xx representation adds a conversion before the player can understand risk.

**Proposal:** show a small causal summary at the point of choice: what changes now,
what develops later, what is unknown, and what investigating can reveal. Name target
changes explicitly. Retain exact costs; use uncertainty ranges only where supported.
Make relevant company, policy and threat details direct links that return to the
same decision. Use the clock as an atmospheric summary with a plain-language risk
label and accessible estimate detail.

Do not merge safety research with oversight just to remove a label: one improves
control capacity, the other improves knowledge/detection. That distinction is a
meaningful strategic tradeoff. Similarly, budget and actions are different limits.

Source: `LabsPanel.tsx`, `NationPanel.tsx`, `Chips.tsx`,
`src/engine/systems/deployment.ts`, `src/engine/preview.ts`.

### 5. The game repeatedly interrupts play to acknowledge and recount

**P1 · Quality defect · Medium confidence for player impact**

Every resolved card opens a separate result overlay. Department actions also open
results. Every quarter opens a report with four meter breakdowns, budget, clock,
warnings, highlights and headlines before returning to the desk. Some information
then appears again in the ticker, desk and panel. The debrief has useful section
navigation, but its seven large section buttons still precede its main content.

Three deterministic traces produced **34–38 cards** and **14–16 quarter reports**.
Reading every card body, evidence line, advisor note, choice label/hint and chosen
outcome would expose **4,862–5,251 English whitespace-delimited words**. This excludes
reports, panel text and most UI labels. At an assumed 200–250 words/minute, that is
roughly **19–26 minutes of reading alone**. This is an illustrative workload estimate,
not a measured completion time: players can skip optional material, and deliberation
would add time. It explains why the nominal 20-minute experience is difficult to
reconcile with exhaustive reading.

The traces include 3–9 situation cards, with Monitor deliberately preferred by the
test policy. That does not prove those cards are redundant. The prior human-style
playtest separately recorded repetitive spillover acknowledgment. Eligibility for
batching must be established case by case.

**Proposal:** keep important outcomes inline with the committed choice, allow the
player to continue at their pace, and retain a history. Quarter summary starts with
the few consequential changes and their causes; exact ledger and headlines expand
on demand. Never auto-dismiss information the player needs to read.

Only batch a threat update if it introduces no new cause, stage, urgency, deadline,
available response or changed consequence, and an existing response still applies.
New escalation must interrupt. Automatically choosing Monitor can change strategy
and is not authorized by a cosmetic simplification.

Evidence: [landscape report](../artifacts/ux-audit-2026-10-12/en-844x390-quarter-report.png),
[ending](../artifacts/ux-audit-2026-10-12/en-390x844-threshold-ending.png).
Source: `Results.tsx`, `QuarterReport.tsx`, `EndingScreen.tsx`.
Workload method and traces: `workload.ts`, `workload.json` in the evidence directory.

### 6. Too many visual treatments perform the same job

**P2 · Slop pattern, with a separate rendering defect · High confidence**

The repeated combination is the problem: network/grid background, scanlines,
glowing cyan buttons, gradient panels, outlines, shadows, tinted pills, tracked
uppercase labels, rough paper, paperclip, tilted sheet, CLASSIFIED stamp and
typewriter type. Several layers signal “serious technology” or “secret document”
at once. Uniform emphasis makes routine items feel as important as a crisis.

On the World screen, the map and four large selector buttons precede the Accord
and tension information. On Programs, the page heading, subtitle, four large
section controls and treasury strip push the first program halfway down the screen.
Many functional labels are 10–12 px, including abbreviated Russian HUD labels.
Reducing body type to make this all fit would worsen the hierarchy.

**Removal test:** remove the background network/grid and scanlines during reading;
remove paper rotation, paperclip and dense grain; remove decorative metadata where
it carries no information; eliminate gradients/glows from ordinary controls; replace
repeated metric pills with aligned text where a badge does not encode a special
state. Keep provenance, actual redactions, cost, confidence, urgency and meaningful
stamps. Use occasional imagery for a major story beat rather than behind every task.

**Separate defect:** the title's SVG wordmark has overlapping/uneven letters in
WebKit after fonts are loaded, while Chromium renders it correctly. The source
combines `textLength`, nested `tspan` and layered copies. That is a place to investigate,
not a proven single cause. Physical WKWebView reproduction remains to be checked.

Evidence: [Programs](../artifacts/ux-audit-2026-10-12/en-390x844-nation-1.png),
[World](../artifacts/ux-audit-2026-10-12/en-390x844-world.png),
[WebKit title](../artifacts/ux-audit-2026-10-12/logo-webkit.png),
[Chromium title](../artifacts/ux-audit-2026-10-12/logo-chromium.png).
Source: `main.css`, `ui-game.css`, `ui-card.css`, `fx.css`, `src/art/logo.ts`.

### 7. Conflicting statements make uncertainty feel like a defect

**P1 · Quality defect · High confidence for the reproduced case**

The Q2 2029 review save shows a Mass Manipulation threat with “Cause identified”,
“Traced to Compass-4 (Orbis Group)” and “Nobody can name a cause.” The narrative
remains in an unknown-cause state after the factual state changes. Source inspection
confirms the phrase is not covered by the current sentence-filter heuristic.

In a game about uncertain evidence, the player must be able to distinguish a
low-confidence report, disagreement between sources and contradictory UI copy.

**Proposal:** author explicit known/unknown/resolved narrative variants, or assemble
state-specific sentences from structured information. Do not rely on an expanding
English/Russian phrase blacklist. Identify whose claim each statement represents;
retain uncertainty when it is real. A neutral “unknown consequences” badge everywhere
does not teach the player which evidence would change their decision.

Evidence: [identified threat with conflicting copy](../artifacts/ux-audit-2026-10-12/en-390x844-turn10-threats.png).
Source: `src/content/situations.ts` mass-manipulation text;
`src/engine/systems/situations.ts` `situationText()`.

### 8. The finale's contract conflicts with the recoverability pillar

**P1 · Game-design risk · High confidence in the rule; player effect needs testing**

The GDD says a bad ending should require a chain of ignored warnings and never one
unlucky roll. The implemented finale resolves control with an RNG roll; even the
maximum probability is capped at 97%. A failure can therefore follow a strong run.
The old playtest's failed 58–75% estimate illustrates the emotional cost, but does
not establish that its policy was optimal or that the probabilities are unbalanced.

The updated debrief explains grades, but explanation alone cannot reconcile the
underlying promise. The introductory manual also emphasizes surviving to the
Threshold and being graded, without clearly teaching that control can still fail
there.

**Two discussable choices:**

1. Keep the gamble and state that contract early, show the known drivers before
   commitment, and distinguish the world outcome from the quality of governance.
2. Change the rule so strong preparation provides a bounded failure or costly
   recovery route, while accumulated failures can still produce catastrophe.

I favor exploring option 2 after the interaction prototype. It requires simulation,
balance work and a deliberate design decision. It must not slip into a UI cleanup.

Source: `docs/GDD.md` pillars; `src/engine/systems/threshold.ts`
`controlChance()` / `resolveThresholdCard()`; `src/engine/endings.ts`.

## What should survive simplification

- The player has to trade growth, political survival and safety. There must not be
  an always-highlighted “correct” answer.
- Paying scarce actions to investigate can change a decision. Keep weak evidence,
  rival estimates, biased advisors and the possibility of missed detection.
- A model, company or policy returns with a consequence several quarters later.
  Preserve names and links that make that causality recognizable.
- Governments and companies push agendas. Shorter copy must preserve those voices.
- The debrief reveals how the run happened and supports another attempt.

The strongest prior examples were revisiting a suspicious model, using an earlier
off-switch policy against a later crisis, and recognizing an investment's later
social cost. A redesign should bring those relationships forward.

## Proposed experience and information structure

### The quarter

Current: desk → long dossier → jump to choices → jump back to evidence → commit →
result overlay → more dossiers → browse multiple management panels → End Quarter →
long report → desk.

Proposed: **what changed → decide the urgent matters → use remaining actions →
review the quarter's consequences.**

The desk prioritizes unresolved crises, due promises and new decisions. The player
can still inspect the world or take a legal action before resolving a briefing;
the suggested order does not become a new rule restriction. The end-quarter control
belongs to the desk, with a visible reminder of unused actions and deadlines.

Try three primary destinations in the prototype:

- **Desk:** decisions, active threats, deadlines and relevant signals; each opens
  the exact item or action.
- **Nation:** funding, laws, society and domestic companies; company/model records
  are directly reachable from any related briefing.
- **World:** rivals, tension and treaty actions, with the map secondary.

This reduces top-level switching but makes some directory navigation one level
deeper. Direct links and task-based testing must justify the trade. Three tabs by
itself is not a success criterion. History is accessible from the desk/menu, with
important outcomes linked from their originating model or threat.

### Proposed wireframes (concepts, not rendered screens)

Portrait decision, approximately 390×844:

```text
‹ Desk                  Q1 2027        Menu
Orbis Group · Deployment request

Should Compass-4 enter business use?
One short paragraph: request, stakes, uncertainty.

What we know                 Evidence ›
Capability: company claim · low confidence
Evaluation: [actual finding and its confidence]

Your options
○ Approve deployment
○ Approve with limits
○ Order a deep evaluation
○ Reject

[Selected option: cost + tradeoff + timing]
Relevant budget/actions and affected meters

             [Confirm named decision]
```

On a short screen, the selected detail can scroll; option labels stay easy to
reach. Costs and disabling reasons are visible before commitment. Do not force
all long text into one screen by shrinking it. Detailed dossier and advisors open
on demand and return to the preserved selection.

Landscape alternative, approximately 844×390:

```text
‹ Desk                Decision title                  Q1 2027
------------------------------------------------------------
Request + stakes             | Options, costs and tradeoffs
Evidence summary             | Selected option detail
Full dossier / Advisors      | [Confirm named decision]
```

Each side has deliberate scrolling behavior with its header/selection preserved.
No full global HUD or global bottom tab row within this view. Prototype it before
promising landscape support. My preference is to qualify portrait first; choosing
portrait-only for the next build is a product decision for this discussion.

Desk and outcome structure:

```text
Quarter + compact national state + resources
Due now / escalating       → relevant decision or action
Briefings awaiting decision → short task list
Remaining actions          → contextual available actions
End Quarter                → consequential changes + causes
                              exact ledger / headlines optional
```

Ending starts with the outcome, the most important causal chain and one meaningful
replay lesson. Scores and hidden truths follow in compact expandable sections.
Keep complete data available without seven equally prominent navigation buttons.

## Feature reduction proposals and consequences

| Feature | Proposed treatment | Consequence / category |
|---|---|---|
| Four survival meters | Compact overview; show affected ones in decisions; critical states always surface | Presentation; do not silently hide impending failure |
| Risk clock | Keep as motif; add readable risk description; full estimate detail on demand | Presentation; preserve fog and ranges |
| Safety vs oversight | Keep separate, explain in the first relevant funding decision | Preserves distinct strategy |
| Five funding programs | Plain-language purpose, current level/upkeep, preview proposed change | Presentation; no automatic reallocations |
| Ten laws | Compact catalog, active/relevant first, searchable or grouped detail later | Presentation; all legal actions remain discoverable |
| Company/model data | Short roster, details by selection, direct contextual links | Presentation; remove repeated full cards from the main path |
| Adoption, targets, dependence, displacement | Explain when relevant; connect target to gradual uptake | Presentation; do not collapse distinct simulated quantities |
| Advisors | Short conflicting positions beside relevant evidence; full commentary optional | Authored content edit; preserve bias and warnings |
| Duplicate alerts, ordinary result overlays | Consolidate while keeping an inspectable record | Interaction change; verify no lost information |
| Monitor updates | Batch only under explicit semantic-equivalence criteria | Can alter decisions; separate review and tests |
| Daily Run, seed and country modifiers | Keep accessible, de-emphasize during the first assignment | Setup presentation; keep replayability |
| 16-quarter campaign | Retain initially; measure comprehension and pacing after redesign | Cutting turns changes arcs, deadlines and balance |
| Finale roll | Discuss recovery/graded failure separately | Explicit game-rule change |

I do not recommend removing mechanics solely because they need an explanation.
First distinguish a useful tradeoff from the work required to find and interpret it.
If newcomers still cannot build a mental model after the contextual redesign,
consider a smaller campaign with its own rules and pacing as a separate product mode.

## Visual direction for discussion

### Recommended: a restrained government dossier

Use the existing document identity more deliberately: clean paper reading surfaces,
dark ink, a matte dark desk frame, clear institutional headings and restrained red
for urgency/decisions. The contemporary subject does not require neon lighting.

- Start with hierarchy, spacing and copy. Choose final typefaces only after English
  and Russian prototypes demonstrate equivalent readability. Two main text roles,
  with tabular numerals where needed, are sufficient for the first exploration.
- Proposed starting sizes: 16–18 px reading text, 13–14 px functional secondary
  text, 24–28 px main decision heading. Validate larger text and smaller devices.
- One document surface per decision. Use whitespace and rules before nested boxes.
- Keep source, date and confidence when useful. Treat file numbers and stamps as
  occasional narrative detail, not repeated visual headers.
- Crisp controls with a clear selected/pressed state. Reserve strong color for
  action priority or game state; label favorable/adverse effects in text too.
- Paper texture should be absent behind reading text or subtle enough to disappear
  at normal viewing distance. No synthetic aging for its own sake.
- Motion communicates reveal, a committed decision or a changed state. Reading text
  appears immediately by default; longer theatrical transitions are skippable.
- Keep the risk clock as a recognizable symbol, subject to a legibility pass.
  Remove decorative representations that compete with the actual choice.

### Alternative: a contemporary operations room

Retain an all-dark interface with flat, solid surfaces, quiet typography, a small
number of charts, source-led evidence and disciplined alert colors. This better
supports players who prefer a technical strategy game, but needs strong task
composition to avoid returning to a generic dashboard. The dossier remains a
structured intelligence record, without distressed paper decoration.

I recommend the first direction because documentary evidence and political
decisions are the game's most distinctive material. A static art sample should
compare these two directions using the *same real deployment*, not an unrelated
hero image. No final palette/font/illustration system is approved yet.

## Proposed sequence after agreement

1. **Interactive decision prototype** — portrait desk, one deployment, evidence,
   selection and outcome; compare a compact landscape version. Estimate 2–3 working
   days including EN/RU and one revision. Review this before applying the design
   across the game.
2. **Phone shell and learning flow** — navigation, contextual links, guided first
   assignment, clear funding/action feedback. Estimate 3–5 days after prototype
   agreement. Test with fresh players while the scope is still small.
3. **Panels, narrative states and recap** — rosters, laws, threats, quarter summary,
   debrief and content corrections. Estimate 3–5 days, depending on authored
   summaries and translations.
4. **Device qualification** — compact/current iPhone, rotation if supported,
   save/resume, VoiceOver/larger text, interruptions and sustained play. Estimate
   2–3 days plus participant/device availability.

Planning range: **10–16 working days** after direction approval. This is not a
delivery promise. Finale redesign, a shortened campaign and new illustrated assets
would need separate estimates. Reuse the existing native shell and deterministic
engine; this proposal does not require a native UI rewrite.

## Acceptance criteria for the redesign

These are proposed tests, not claims already achieved.

- The decision's question, all option labels and how to inspect evidence are visible
  or directly reachable without reading the entire dossier. 390×844 baseline;
  320×568 and Russian remain usable without shrinking functional text below target.
- In landscape, if supported, task content occupies at least 70% of the usable
  height after system safe areas. No center slice with 85 px of content.
- Essential controls have at least 44×44 logical-pixel hit areas at normal WKWebView
  scale, then are verified on device. Reading order and focus survive detail views.
- In a formative test with five people unfamiliar with the game, at least four
  complete a first briefing without coaching and can explain: the request, the
  selected tradeoff, the cost and what remains unknown.
- After the guided assignment, at least four can explain why low-confidence “no
  issues found” does not establish safety and why adoption does not jump directly
  to its target. This is a small directional sample, not statistical validation.
- From a promise or threat, players reach the relevant information/action in at
  most two navigation steps and return without losing their decision context.
- Record time spent reading, navigating, deciding and dismissing separately. Seek
  a clear reduction in navigation/acknowledgment without requiring faster decisions.
  Validate the eventual session target with humans, not automated run duration.
- No signal, cost, blocked reason or material warning disappears when a summary
  replaces detailed presentation. Verify EN/RU and every card kind, including long
  names, four options, crises and the finale.
- A content consistency check covers unknown → suspected → identified → resolved
  threats. No contradictory cause statements.
- Full device save, resume and update checks remain part of release readiness.

## Decisions for our discussion

1. **Primary experience:** agree on portrait-first play; decide whether the next
   iteration should lock to portrait or also qualify a purpose-built landscape view.
2. **Visual identity:** choose the restrained dossier direction or the contemporary
   operations-room alternative for the prototype.
3. **Depth:** start by reducing presentation/learning burden while keeping the
   strategic systems, or explicitly commission a smaller ruleset as well.
4. **Finale:** retain the disclosed gamble, or separately explore costly recovery
   and a less binary outcome for strong preparation.

**The single highest-impact correction is to give the active decision the full
screen and bring its stakes, evidence and options into one coherent view.**
